TEACHING

Research-led teaching for the hydrogen transition.

I bring materials science, electrochemistry, practical experimentation and current hydrogen research into the learning environment, helping students connect engineering fundamentals with real technologies and challenges.

TEACHING PHILOSOPHY

Active, inclusive and research-informed learning.

My teaching approach is centred on helping students understand not only the principles behind hydrogen technologies, but also how those principles are applied in real engineering and research environments. I combine concise lectures with active learning, tutorials, practical activities and research-led examples.

I use a blended approach that incorporates flipped-learning activities, short pre-recorded materials and continuous feedback. This allows classroom time to focus on discussion, problem-solving and deeper engagement, whilst recognising that students arrive with different academic and professional backgrounds.

My aim is to create an environment in which students feel supported to ask questions, challenge ideas, develop confidence and progressively become independent learners and researchers.

TEACHING EXPERIENCE

From materials science to hydrogen technologies.

I have more than a decade of teaching and academic supervision experience across undergraduate and postgraduate education in Brazil and the UK. At the University of Birmingham, I have contributed to Introduction to Hydrogen Technologies and Hydrogen and Hydrogen-Based Fuels, and led teaching for High-Temperature Fuel Cells.

At UFRJ, I taught Principles of Materials Science, Materials Characterisation and Phase Transformations, alongside specialist fuel-cell teaching. These experiences have allowed me to teach across different levels, from fundamental materials concepts to advanced electrochemical technologies.

ACTIVE & PRACTICAL LEARNING

Connecting theory with the laboratory.

I use practical research examples to help students understand how materials, electrochemistry and operating conditions influence real devices. Where appropriate, teaching is connected to laboratory work, experimental data, electrochemical testing and the challenges encountered during research.

For flipped-learning activities, students can engage with short recorded material before class, whilst classroom sessions are used for questions, discussion, tutorials and applied problem-solving. I also use continuous assessment techniques such as traffic-light feedback and one-minute feedback to identify areas that need further explanation.

INCLUSIVE TEACHING

Supporting different learners and backgrounds.

Hydrogen programmes bring together students with very different academic and professional backgrounds. I therefore aim to make complex technical material accessible without reducing its depth, using multiple ways of explaining concepts and creating opportunities for students to check their understanding.

I also pay attention to accessibility and individual circumstances. For recorded teaching, I review and correct automatic subtitles and use accessibility tools such as Microsoft Office Accessibility Checker when preparing teaching materials.

SUPERVISION & MENTORING

Helping students become independent researchers.

I have supervised undergraduate, Master's and PhD students in materials, fuel-cell and hydrogen research. My supervision combines technical guidance with increasing student independence: defining research questions, planning and conducting experiments, analysing results, troubleshooting, and communicating findings through dissertations and presentations.

I have also worked with students from different countries and academic backgrounds. Four peer-reviewed publications have resulted from research involving students I supervised, with further work currently under review.

Beyond the formal research project, I see supervision as an opportunity to help students develop confidence, scientific judgement and transferable skills that support their future careers.

RESEARCH-LED EDUCATION

Bringing an international hydrogen network into teaching.

I see teaching and research as mutually reinforcing. Through my international hydrogen research network, I will create opportunities for students to engage with academics, researchers and industry partners beyond the university, including guest lectures, seminars, recorded contributions and internationally connected student projects.

I am also the lead author and editor of Solid Oxide Cells: From Fundamentals to Applications, one of 11 hydrogen-focused textbooks being developed through the educational activities associated with HyAcademy.eu, with co-authors from the UK, Europe and Brazil. This work reflects my commitment to making specialist hydrogen education more accessible and internationally connected.
